# Marketing, launch and retention

## Positioning to test

Changeo helps people find someone for a particular or unusual job, and helps providers be discovered for the work they can actually do. Both sides can start the process through **Necesito** or **Ofrezco**. Freeform service names and local/remote/shipped fulfillment support the vision without requiring every job to fit a narrow directory category.

Do not claim there are no competitors or that payment protection is unique. [Workana](https://help.workana.com/hc/es/articles/360041401574-Dep%C3%B3sito-en-Garant%C3%ADa-Escrow) already describes protected fixed-price projects, and [Zolvers](https://zolvers.com/) addresses local service demand. Validate the combined Changeo proposition through interviews and eventual observed use.

## Acquisition sequence

1. Resolve payment/business feasibility before promising protection or committing a major launch campaign.
2. Recruit real providers and interested customers through the founder's network, relevant communities and partnerships. An optional prelaunch interest list supports recruitment, not a public product prototype.
3. Record provider capabilities, fulfillment reach, availability and genuine demand examples. Obtain consent for portfolios and promotional work examples.
4. Use actual supply/demand overlap to choose campaign audiences while keeping national signup and broad permitted services open.
5. Make one public production launch after payment, product, moderation and support acceptance.
6. Expand paid campaigns in funded tranches based on availability, conversion and support capacity.

Heavy marketing investment is an explicit user intention. This plan does not replace it with organic-only acquisition. It separates substantial funding from releasing an unmeasured budget all at once.

## Channel plan

| Channel | Initial objective | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder recruitment | Seed credible, available providers and understand unmet requests. | Activated providers, response quality and real job opportunities. |
| Community partnerships | Reach makerspaces, trade groups, universities, clubs and relevant local businesses. | Qualified signups and completed jobs attributable to each relationship. |
| Search advertising | Capture concrete service needs where provider supply exists. | First completed-job acquisition cost and unanswered-demand rate. |
| Social advertising | Demonstrate genuine use cases and recruit both sides. | Qualified demand/provider activation, not only clicks or registrations. |
| Public service pages/SEO | Make approved relevant offers discoverable. | Relevant visits, contacts, funded jobs and duplicate/thin-page quality. |
| Shareable requests/profiles | Help users distribute a real need or capability. | Suitable responses, conversion and privacy-safe sharing. |
| Referrals/retention | Encourage successful users to return and invite others. | Incremental completed jobs, abuse rate, reward cost and repeat retention. |

Respect community rules and permissions; do not create fake listings, reviews or unsolicited automated outreach. Campaign publishing, external messages, partnerships and paid spending require actual authorization, separate from writing this plan.

## Campaign hypotheses

Provider messages can test founding membership, finding customers for a specialized capability, and transparent subscription benefits. Customer messages can test difficult-to-find work, a simple request process, and clearly described funded-agreement protections once those protections exist.

Landing pages should reflect the minimal product style: one concrete promise, real examples and the relevant next action. Avoid crowded feature lists and promises of guaranteed safety, guaranteed work or guaranteed income.

## Measurement

Track privacy-compliant attribution through:

```text
visit -> qualified signup -> available offer/request -> suitable response
      -> accepted agreement -> funded job -> completed settlement -> repeat use
```

Measure provider and customer acquisition separately. Provider CAC is based on activated and ultimately paying providers; customer CAC uses first completed paid jobs. Report paid and blended acquisition costs, including founder effort where practical. Separate free founders, trials and paid cohorts.

Track response time, unanswered requests, proposal acceptance, funding/completion, cancellations, disputes, review delays, repeat jobs, provider responsiveness, subscription renewal and churn. Break down results by geography, fulfillment mode and service wording without requiring a rigid category taxonomy.

Retention features support actual successful work: reusable offers, notification relevance, job history, repeat contact and understandable reward progress. Earned visibility must not pretend to be neutral reputation or undermine match quality.

## Budget and expansion gates

The founder approves channel budgets, runway and stop/expand thresholds after the [business model](business.md) includes processing subsidies, moderation and support. No budget or CAC was supplied, so none is fabricated here.

Before releasing a tranche, set numeric targets from the financial model for suitable-response rate, completed-job conversion, contribution, retention, fraud and support capacity. If a campaign attracts demand that cannot be served, correct coverage rather than buying more unfulfilled requests. Do not fund acquisition from customer money awaiting release.
